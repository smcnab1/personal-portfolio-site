## Context

Healthcare learners need to practise complex decisions before they meet them in clinical care. Good simulation makes that possible without pretending that realism alone guarantees learning.

## Problem

A technically impressive scenario can still exclude learners, overload them or fail to connect with practice. The design has to account for learning outcomes, psychological safety, accessibility and the realities of clinical work.

## Role

As Senior Lecturer in Simulation & Immersive Technologies, I design and lead scenario-based learning across undergraduate and postgraduate healthcare education.

## Approach

I combine high-fidelity environments, immersive technologies and structured debriefing with a deliberately inclusive approach. The scenario, facilitation and follow-up are treated as one learning system.

My clinical background helps keep the details credible; my experience as a neurodivergent educator keeps attention on clarity, cognitive load and different ways of participating.

## Outcome

The work supports learners to rehearse clinical reasoning, communication and teamwork in realistic settings. It remains an evolving area of practice rather than a single finished product.

## Methods

- Scenario and curriculum design
- High-fidelity and immersive simulation
- Facilitation and structured debriefing
- Inclusive learning design
- Clinical and educational evaluation
