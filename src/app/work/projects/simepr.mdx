## Context

Electronic patient records shape modern healthcare, yet learners do not always have a safe, realistic place to practise using them before placement.

## Problem

Paper exercises cannot fully reproduce the decisions involved in navigating a record, interpreting information and documenting care. Access to live clinical systems is neither appropriate nor scalable for routine teaching.

## Role

I led implementation work using SimEPR within undergraduate healthcare education at the University of West London, connecting the platform with simulation and curriculum needs.

## Approach

The work placed a simulated electronic patient record inside realistic learning activities. Learners could review information, document decisions and experience digital workflow without using real patient data.

## Outcome

SimEPR created a practical bridge between classroom learning, simulation and digitally enabled practice. The implementation also informed a conference presentation on simulated electronic patient records in undergraduate mental health nursing.

## Methods

- Digital simulation design
- Curriculum integration
- Faculty and learner support
- Safe demonstration data
- Evaluation and dissemination
