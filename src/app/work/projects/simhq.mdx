## Context

Simulation centres coordinate rooms, equipment, people, support and a steady stream of small operational decisions. Those workflows are often spread across generic booking tools, inboxes and local spreadsheets.

## Problem

The work is specialised, but the software rarely is. That creates duplicated admin, unclear ownership and avoidable friction around the learning itself.

## Role

I founded SimHQ and lead its product direction and development. The work brings together my experience in simulation education, clinical practice and software.

## Approach

SimHQ is being developed as a modular platform rather than one oversized system. Publicly described modules include Rooms for simulation-centre operations, Support for help and feedback, and Assets for equipment workflows.

The product is shaped around real tasks, clear permissions and a low-friction experience for both occasional and experienced users.

## Current status

SimHQ is in active development, with Rooms available in alpha. The public site describes current availability without presenting planned modules as finished.

## Methods

- Product discovery grounded in simulation-centre practice
- Accessible web application design
- Modular product and content architecture
- Iterative delivery and operational feedback
