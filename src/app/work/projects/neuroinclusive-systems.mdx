## Context

Many education and workplace systems quietly assume one way of organising, communicating and concentrating. Neurodivergent people often carry the cost of navigating those assumptions.

## Problem

Inclusion can remain abstract: a policy, awareness session or list of adjustments that never changes the everyday experience of learning and work.

## Role

I bring lived experience as a neurodivergent educator to staff-network leadership, teaching practice, software decisions and institutional conversations.

## Approach

I focus on low-friction changes: clearer structures, fewer hidden expectations, accessible choices and systems that do not demand unnecessary executive function. The aim is not to design a separate experience, but a better default.

## Outcome

This is continuing advocacy and practice rather than a completed campaign. Its value is visible in the questions asked, the barriers removed and the space created for colleagues and students to work differently.

## Methods

- Neuro-affirming learning design
- Staff advocacy and peer support
- Accessible communication
- Universal design
- Reflective practice
